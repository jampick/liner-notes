# Liner Notes: heuristic brief

A Liner Notes journey starts from an idea (a genre, a writer, a producer, a city, a scene) and a panel argues the songs into a list. Before the panel sits, someone writes down what's in and what's out. That's the heuristic, and it's what you draft here. The person who started the journey edits it, and the panel judges every candidate against it, so it has to be specific enough to settle an argument.

## What to write

Plain Markdown with these sections, each a short paragraph or a few lines. Say where the line falls and give a named example on each side of it when you can.

- Era: the years the journey covers, and why those bounds. Name what happened at each end.
- Personnel: the people whose involvement puts a record in (writers, producers, session players, singers), and how much involvement counts. A co-write? A credit on one track of an album?
- Sound: what it sounds like, in concrete terms: instruments, tempo, production, arrangement. Name two or three records that sit dead centre.
- Themes: what the songs are about, if that matters to the idea.
- The one-recording rule: which version of a song counts when there are several (the original, the hit, the remake), and how to pick.
- Out: the near misses and why. The neighbouring genre, the famous record that doesn't fit, the late cash-in.

Facts in the heuristic are checked like any other: real names, real years. If you're not sure of one, say so in the text rather than guess.
